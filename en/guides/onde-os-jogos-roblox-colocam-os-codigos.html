Where Roblox games hide their code menus

We compared the redemption instructions from 34 games in the 67Codes catalog to identify the menus, requirements, and unusual systems players encounter most often.

YoiteFounder and editorPublished November 23, 2026Updated November 23, 2026

What we analyzed

We reviewed the redemption instructions from the 34 games in our catalog that currently have a code field, have used one recently, or provide a different official redemption method. The goal was not to create a universal path—Roblox does not control these menus—but to identify the interface patterns that appear repeatedly.

The comparison also shows why a code can be correct while the player still cannot find the field. Some developers place redemption behind a shop, a tutorial, account progress, a physical location, or a command that does not resemble a normal Codes button.

The shop is the most common starting point

Shop and Store menus appear repeatedly in our instructions. Volleyball Legends places codes inside the shop, Ghost Driver uses Shop followed by Rewards or Codes, and games such as Runaways, Fish It!, Anime Dice, and DOORS require the player to open a store and sometimes scroll before the field becomes visible.

When a store has several tabs, look for Rewards, Codes, Gift, or a text field near the bottom. Scrolling matters: a field placed below products can be missed on a small screen even when the correct menu is already open.

  • Open Shop or Store and inspect every tab.
  • Scroll to the bottom before leaving the menu.
  • Check Rewards and Gift sections even when there is no tab named Codes.

Dedicated icons are faster, but not always obvious

Several experiences expose redemption directly through an icon. Dress To Impress uses the bag or codes icon on the left, Anime Vanguards and Dungeon Lootr use dedicated code controls, and Speed Keyboard Escape places its button near the top-left corner.

The symbol may look like a gift, ticket, social-media logo, or generic menu button. This is why the instructions on each game page describe the location and visual context instead of telling every player to search for the same icon.

Requirements that can hide or block redemption

A missing field does not always mean that the feature was removed. Fish an Anime RNG asks the player to finish its introductory tutorial, while TYPE SOUL requires account progress before its gift control can be used. RIVALS may ask for initial tasks or an official follow requirement before a reward is released.

Other systems are completely different. Adopt Me uses a redemption kiosk in a specific map location, Catalog Avatar Creator accepts a chat command, and Pet Simulator 99 distinguishes individual product codes from free public codes. These cases should not be forced into the usual Shop-and-Redeem instructions.

A practical search order

If you have a confirmed code but no instructions, first inspect the main interface for a Codes or gift icon. Then check Shop, Settings, Menu, and Rewards, including content below the visible area. If the field remains unavailable, complete the tutorial and read any requirement shown near the button.

Use the specific game page whenever possible. A precise route is safer than assuming every Roblox experience follows the same menu structure, especially after an interface update.

  • Dedicated Codes, gift, or ticket icon.
  • Shop or Store, including Rewards tabs and the bottom of the page.
  • Settings or the main menu.
  • Tutorial, rank, group, or location requirements.
  • Alternative official methods such as a kiosk or chat command.
